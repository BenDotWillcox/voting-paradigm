What the proposed forest connects

Undersized components may connect to one another, but every resulting tree must reach an eligible root. Root-to-root connections are excluded.

A connected graph and a feasible population total are necessary checks. They do not prove that the whole-block solver will produce balanced, connected districts.

Population constraints change with the district count

The 99% threshold classifies source components; it does not promise 1% final population balance. Bounds below treat population as freely divisible within each proposed tree, making them optimistic. Counts above 4 are illustrations, outside the requested House-size anchors.

Large enough for a district is not an assignment

The current rule protects components whose population can support at least one district from being linked to another eligible root. A root can still be divided into several districts; it is not automatically made a single district.

A district using a water connection must own both endpoint blocks and connect its assigned polygon parts through shared edges plus its recorded links. A nearby island cannot borrow another district’s route.

When the same policy stops being enough

If two eligible roots remain separate, their trees must each receive a whole number of districts. Even two individually large islands can have population totals that do not fit whole statewide district quotas.

That is an explicit trade-off between keeping large islands separate and equal population. It requires a component-aware allocation check; silently connecting large roots would change the policy.
